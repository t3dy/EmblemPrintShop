### 1. Leiden Papyrus X (Papyrus Graecus Leidensis I 397)
**Date:** Late 3rd – early 4th century AD  
**Provenance:** Discovered ~1828 near Thebes, Egypt; sold 1829 by Johan d'Anastasy to the Rijksmuseum  
**Repository:** Rijksmuseum van Oudheden (National Museum of Antiquities), Leiden, Netherlands  
**Inventory no.:** P. Leid. I 397 (Trismegistos TM 61300)  
**Content:** 111 Greek recipes for metallurgical/alchemical procedures — purification of base metals, alloys imitating gold/silver. One of the oldest surviving alchemical documents. **Not illustrated** (workshop technical document); no images.  
**Companion:** Stockholm Papyrus (Papyrus Graecus Holmiensis), Royal Library of Sweden — parallel document, also unillustrated.  
**Digitization:** RMO online collection; Trismegistos database: https://www.trismegistos.org/text/61300  
**Status:** ❌ No images to extract (text-only papyrus)  
**Note for corpus:** Could source a photograph of the physical papyrus from RMO if a material-artifact record is wanted.

### 2. Codex Marcianus gr. Z. 299 (Chrysopoeia of Cleopatra & Ouroboros)
**Date:** 10th–11th century Byzantine copy of late-antique originals (~3rd–4th century AD)  
**Provenance:** Donated to Venice 1468 by Cardinal Bessarion with his Byzantine manuscript collection  
**Repository:** Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, Venice  
**Shelfmark:** MS Marciana gr. Z. 299 (coll. 584)  
**Physical:** ~300 parchment folios  

**Key images:**
- **fol. 188v** — *Chrysopoeia of Cleopatra*: The earliest surviving ouroboros diagram, a serpent biting its own tail encircling the Greek text "ἓν τὸ πᾶν" (*hen to pan*, "the All is One"). The single most important image in the history of alchemy. Associated with Cleopatra the Alchemist (3rd–4th c. AD).
- **fol. 21v** — Zosimos of Panopolis alchemical apparatus diagrams (kerotakis, distillation still, tribikos) — among the earliest surviving technical illustrations in Western science
- Several folios — Zosimos visions / "Book of Sophe" text with diagrams
- Stephanus of Alexandria, pseudo-Democritus, Hermes fragments

**Digitization:** ✅ Available via Biblissima IIIF portal (Mirador viewer):  
https://portail.biblissima.fr/ark:/43093/mdataf361a5cb77a866280bc1a1e50cf143dd8416e045  
Princeton Byzantine Sources index: https://byzantine.lib.princeton.edu/byzantine/manuscripts/30390  

**Status:** 🔴 **PRIORITY — NEEDS SOURCING.** The ouroboros (fol. 188v) is the foundational image of Western alchemy. Use Biblissima IIIF to download the folio image directly.  
**Download approach:** IIIF manifest → individual canvas for fol. 188v. Fetch via manifest URL, identify canvas for fol. 188v, download at full resolution.

### 8. Ripley Scroll (~1490–1624)
**Attribution:** George Ripley (c. 1415–1490), Canon of Bridlington; all surviving scrolls are copies  
**Date:** Copies late 16th – early 17th century (none contemporaneous with Ripley)  
**Language:** English (Middle/Early Modern)  
**Physical:** Up to 5.5–6 meters long × ~50 cm wide; a continuous visual narrative  
**Content:** One of the most extraordinary objects in alchemical iconography — a single scroll depicting the alchemical process as a continuous sequence of symbolic scenes: a crowned toad bled by three serpents, the Green Lion swallowing the sun, a pelican, a phoenix, vessels and furnaces, kings in putrefaction, the resurrection of the Stone. 23–28 copies survive in major libraries.

### 9. Nicolas Flamel — Livre des Figures Hiéroglyphiques (14th–17th c.)
**Historical note:** The historical Nicolas Flamel (c. 1330–1418) was a Paris scrivener; the alchemical writings attributed to him are almost entirely pseudepigraphal, composed from the 16th century onward. The "Cemetery of the Innocents" sculptures he purportedly carved no longer survive (cemetery demolished 1786). The *Livre des Figures Hiéroglyphiques* purports to describe 22 hieroglyphic paintings from that cemetery.  
**Content:** 22 "hieroglyphic figures" allegedly painted on the arches of the Cemetery of the Innocents: an angel with a book, winged Mercury, Adam and Eve with serpent, dragons devouring each other, a king commanding soldiers to massacre children (Herod's Massacre = alchemical purification of metals). First printed 1612 Paris.

### 11. Rosarium Philosophorum — 1550 Frankfurt First Edition (Woodcut Version)
**Author:** Anonymous  
**Publication:** First printed as Part II of *De Alchimia Opuscula complura veterum philosophorum* (Frankfurt: Cyriacus Jacobi, 1550)  
**Content:** 20 woodcuts with German captions depicting the ten-stage coniunctio of Sol and Luna — the "royal marriage" sequence: the king and queen meet, disrobe, conjoin in the bath, die together, putrefy, and resurrect as the *Rebis*. These 20 woodcuts are the canonical visual vocabulary for conjunction iconography. No pre-1550 MS version is known; this print is the ur-text.  
**Note:** Our current corpus has a later IA scan. This entry tracks the availability of the original 1550 Frankfurt woodcuts at higher resolution.

### 12. Basil Valentine — Azoth (1613 Frankfurt, Lucas Jennis)
**Attribution:** "Basil Valentine" is a pseudonym; the *Azoth* material first appeared 1613, Frankfurt. Earlier texts attributed to Valentine (1602 Erfurt) are unillustrated.  
**Full title:** *Azoth, sive Aureliae Occultae Philosophorum* (Frankfurt: Lucas Jennis, 1613)  
**Content:** 10 process emblems plus the central *Azoth mandala* — a circular diagram showing: a seven-branched philosophical tree with two figures (Senior Adolphus) conversing at its base, three roots (Salt, Sulphur, Mercury), seven branches (seven planets/metals), and the *Rebis* (hermaphroditic figure) at center. This mandala is among the most reproduced images in alchemical iconography.

### 14. Mutus Liber (Altus [Isaac Baulot], La Rochelle, 1677)
**Author:** "Altus" (attribution); scholarly consensus identifies **Isaac Baulot**  
**Publisher:** Pierre Savouret, La Rochelle, 1677  
**Physical:** 15 engraved plates plus title page; entirely wordless (except motto and title)  
**Content:** The only major alchemical work composed entirely of images with no explanatory text. 15 plates depicting the alchemical Work through a husband-and-wife laboratory pair: gathering dew, performing operations with flasks, angels descending, the sun and moon phases, the culmination of the Work. The title means "the silent book" (*mutus* = mute/silent).  
**Surviving first-edition copies:** ~12 known in major Western libraries

### 16. Robert Fludd — Utriusque Cosmi Historia (1617–1621)
**Author:** Robert Fludd (1574–1637)  
**Publisher:** Johann-Theodor de Bry, Oppenheim/Frankfurt, 1617–1621  
**Engravers:** J.T. de Bry and **Matthäus Merian the Elder**  
**Physical:** Two folio volumes, ~1,038 pp. total; extensive copperplate engraving program  
**Content:** Fludd's macrocosm/microcosm cosmology with the most ambitious visual program in early modern natural philosophy. Images range from cosmological diagrams to musical harmony, medicine, and astrology.  

**Key images:**
- *Integrae Naturae Speculum* ("Mirror of Nature's Entirety") — divine figure as *anima mundi* spanning the cosmic spheres, a chain from minerals to God
- *Monochord of the Universe* (*Monochordum Mundi*) — a single string stretched from Earth to God, calibrating celestial intervals to musical ratios; one of the most famous scientific/mystical diagrams ever made
- *Pyramides Lucis et Umbrae* — light and shadow pyramids interpenetrating the spheres
- Ptolemaic cosmological sphere diagrams
- Alchemical weather apparatus / "weather glass"
- The dark primordial square (*Et sic in infinitum*) — representing divine darkness before creation
- Music and harmony diagrams
- Geomancy figures

**Digitization:**
- Internet Archive (primary, two-volume set): `utriusquecosmima01flud` → https://archive.org/details/utriusquecosmima01flud  
- Additional IA copies: `bub_gb_pCXwduv541wC` (1619 vol.); `bub_gb_1t2kO04MCQcC` (1617 vol.)  
- Science History Institute (34 high-res plate images): https://digital.sciencehistory.org/works/fvm4tyd  
- Wellcome Collection (1624 Frankfurt reissue, 9 images): https://wellcomecollection.org/works/tymqmuxa  
**Status:** 🔴 **PRIORITY — NEEDS SOURCING.** The SHI digitization gives 34 high-quality plates. IA has the complete volumes for bulk download. The *Monochord* and *Integrae Naturae Speculum* are essential alchemical/Hermetic images.

### 18. Michael Maier — Viatorium (1618)

**Publisher:** Ex typographia Hieronymi Galleri : Sumptibus Joh. Theodori de Bry, Oppenheim, 1618  
**Content:** Allegorical journey through the seven mountains of the planets. Each mountain associated with a metal/planet, with mythological stories and symbolic etchings. 7 planetary mountain etchings.  

**Key plates:**
- Mercury: the seven gates of Thebes
- Saturn: dragon vs. elephant combat
- Moon: Magellan's circumnavigation as allegory
- Sun: the grateful lion (lion extracts thorn from its paw as alchemical metaphor)

**Digitization:**
- Internet Archive (1618 first edition, confirmed): `majeriviatoriumh00maie` → https://archive.org/details/majeriviatoriumh00maie  
- Second edition (1651): `bub_gb_iBW59n4hCCsC`  
**Status:** 🟡 **WORTH SOURCING.** IA identifier confirmed; 7 planetary etchings. Completes the Maier visual corpus beyond *Atalanta Fugiens*.

### 22. Andreas Libavius — Alchymia (1606, 2nd enlarged edition)
**Author:** Andreas Libavius (c. 1555–1616)  
**Publisher:** J. Saurius, Frankfurt, 1606 (the 1597 first edition is unillustrated)  
**Content:** The first systematic chemistry textbook. Contains 200+ woodcuts including:  
- **Domus Chymici** ("the alchemical house") — an ideal laboratory building with floor plan and elevation: perhaps the first published architectural plan for a scientific laboratory
- Dozens of furnace design variants
- Glassware: alembics, retorts, cucurbites, pelicans, distillation apparatus
- "The alchemist at work" scenes

**Digitization:**
- Internet Archive (BIUSanté Paris copy): `BIUSante_00180` → https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_00180  
- Additional IA copy (*Commentariorum Alchemiae*): `bub_gb_0WfRikJt9yQC`  
- Wellcome Collection: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/fpwfjtjb  
- Linda Hall Library (reproduction of Domus Chymici): https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/andreas-libavius/  
**Status:** 🔴 **PRIORITY — NEEDS SOURCING.** The apparatus woodcuts (200+) are exactly what the Equipment extraction pass was designed for — furnaces, alembics, retorts, crucibles. The *Domus Chymici* plan is a landmark image. IA has the complete 1606 edition.

### 24. Geheime Figuren der Rosenkreuzer (Altona, 1785–1788)
**Note:** Technically 18th century, but directly synthesizes and reproduces 17th-century Rosicrucian and alchemical visual traditions; widely used as a primary source for that iconography.  
**Publisher:** Anonymous (Altona, 1785–1788), issued in three pamphlets (*Hefte*)  
**Content:** Elaborate hand-colored folding plates including:
- Cosmological "Atom of Eternity" diagrams
- Philosophical Egg cross-sections (showing the four elements, the Stone)
- Tree of Life diagrams combining Kabbalah and alchemy
- Zodiacal and elemental charts
- Rosicrucian "grade" emblems with planetary symbolism

**Digitization:**
- Internet Archive (all three Hefte): `Geheime-Figuren-der-Rosenkreuzer_3-Hefte_Altona-1785-1788` → https://archive.org/details/Geheime-Figuren-der-Rosenkreuzer_3-Hefte_Altona-1785-1788  
- Also IA: `GeheimeFigurenDerRosenkreuzer1785`; `GeheimeFigurenDerRosenkreutz`

- IAPSOP (PDF): http://iapsop.com/ssoc/1785__anonymous___geheime_figuren_der_rosenkreuzer____v1.pdf
